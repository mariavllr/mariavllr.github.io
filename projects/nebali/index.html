Final degree project · Universitat Jaume I · 2023

Nebali: The Lost Island

This game is part of my final degree project, titled “Immersion in video games and mental health narratives”. The project shows how a video game can be fun and enjoyable while subtly exploring a story centred on mental health. The protagonist, Mateo, is shipwrecked on an unknown island, where he discovers he is not alone: he has to talk to the islanders, complete quests and face enemies to free the island from the “Boos”. I developed the whole project myself in Unity.

My role
Solo developer: design, writing, programming, 3D art, shaders and UI
Context
Final degree project, BSc in Game Design and Development, Universitat Jaume I
Built with
Unity (URP), C#, Blender, Clip Studio Paint

The idea

Mateo wakes up on an unknown island after a shipwreck. To find his way home he has to free it from the Boos, small creatures that stand for intrusive thoughts, and on the way he meets the islanders.

I wanted a game that deals with mental health without being a therapy game: fun for anyone, with characters you can recognise yourself in. For my final degree project I also wanted to explore the cosy aesthetic and model characters and environments from scratch, so I chose this project to apply everything I had learned during my studies.

From sketch to game

Every character started as a pencil sketch. I modelled them all in Blender: hair built from curves, clothes cut from real sewing patterns and fitted with cloth simulation, and one shared body for all the islanders, so a new character only needs new clothes and hair.

Shaders

A node-based dialogue system

The game is built around conversations, which have several choices and can branch. So I built a tool to write them. It's an editor window inside Unity, made with the GraphView API, where each node holds a character and a line and can branch into several choices.

  • Graphs are saved and loaded as ScriptableObject assets that any script can read.
  • A blackboard holds variables, such as names, that any line can use.
  • A parser plays the graph in game: text appears like a typewriter and the choices show up when the line ends.
  • Each character speaks with short beeps at a random pitch inside their own range, so everyone has a voice of their own.

The island

Interface and 2D art

I drew all the interface in Clip Studio Paint to look like an old tale: papyrus dialogues, hand-drawn buttons and item icons.

My work

  • All the gameplay code in C#: player controller, Cinemachine camera, combat and enemies, quests, inventory, diary, map and a fishing minigame, connected through events.
  • The dialogue editor and its in-game parser, plus writing all the dialogue in the story.
  • Toon and water shaders in URP Shader Graph.
  • Design of every character and most of the props, modelled and textured in Blender.
  • Performance work for a large terrain: LODs and occlusion culling.

Results

  • A complete, playable demo with a beginning and an end, three characters with side quests, and enemies.
  • Presented at the Congreso Español de Videojuegos (CEV).
